Chapter 11 — Honest Assessment

Challenges & Limitations

An honest engineering evaluation: what didn't work as planned, what we underestimated, and what the hard physical limits of the current design are. Understanding these is as important as the successes.

01

Structural Stiffness — The Dominant Problem

This is the most significant technical limitation of the current design. The printed PLA link bodies deflect under their own weight and payload — particularly the shoulder and elbow links at maximum reach. Measured deflection at maximum extension under 500g payload: 1.8mm at TCP.

The root cause is the stiffness-to-mass ratio of FDM-printed parts. Hollow or lattice-infill structures achieve good mass savings but sacrifice bending stiffness. At full reach, the second area moment of inertia (I) of our 40×25mm box section is insufficient to hold TCP error below 0.5mm under full payload.

Severity: HIGH

Mitigation: Static gravity compensation model reduces this to ~0.4mm. Long-term fix: carbon fiber reinforcement or all-aluminum links. Both add cost and complexity.

02

Singularity Avoidance in Real Toolpaths

The analytical singularity avoidance works well for simple test paths but fails on complex organic geometries. When non-planar slicers generate paths over highly curved surfaces, they frequently command the nozzle to orientations near J5=0° (wrist singularity) — causing sudden joint velocity spikes and brief print speed drops.

Detected singularity encounters during testing: 23 events in a 45-minute complex print. Each caused a 1–3 second speed reduction. The printed surface shows faint speed-change artifacts at each of these locations.

Severity: MEDIUM

Mitigation: Global path optimization pre-processing (currently in development) will re-orient the entire print to minimize wrist singularity encounters during slicing.

03

Software Integration Complexity

The multi-stage pipeline (slicer → Python motion server → Marlin → hardware) creates numerous integration failure points. Real failures observed during development:

  • Serial buffer overflow during high-density toolpaths caused frame drops → missed steps

  • IK solver returning NaN at workspace boundary → arm moved to home position mid-print

  • Raspberry Pi thermal throttling under sustained IK computation → trajectory timing errors

Severity: MEDIUM

All three bugs were found and fixed, but this highlights the debugging difficulty of a multi-layer software stack with hardware in the loop.

04

Print Speed — Significantly Slower Than Cartesian

The 6-axis arm prints at a maximum practical speed of 40mm/s TCP, compared to 80–150mm/s for a modern Cartesian printer. Three compounding factors:

  • Joint velocity limits: Angular velocity limits map to lower linear TCP speeds at larger reach radii

  • IK computation latency: 12ms/segment on Raspberry Pi limits trajectory update rate

  • Vibration: Above 50mm/s, motor excitation causes ringing artifacts on surfaces

Severity: HIGH (for production use)

Speed is not a blocker for the research objectives. A 20cm-tall vase takes ~3.5 hours vs ~1.5 hours on Cartesian — acceptable for prototyping, not for manufacturing.

Additional Known Limitations

Open-Loop Motors

No position feedback encoders on the stepper axes. Missed steps from electrical noise or mechanical shock cause cumulative position error that grows through the print with no way to detect or correct it.

No Heated Enclosure

Open frame design cannot print ABS or PC reliably due to warping from ambient temperature gradients. Limits the material palette to PLA and PETG for current hardware.

Non-Planar Slicer Gap

No mature, fully automated non-planar slicer exists yet. Current workflow requires manual toolpath specification for curved surfaces — a significant barrier to usability for non-experts.

Lessons Learned

Design for stiffness first, mass second

We optimized for minimum mass early in the design, inadvertently under-sizing the link cross-sections. For a 3D printing robot, accuracy matters more than maximum speed — a stiffer (heavier) arm with better TCP accuracy would have been the right trade-off.

Closed-loop control is not optional for robot printing

Stepper motors work in Cartesian printers because the gantry mechanics are inherently stiff and over-damped. A serial chain arm amplifies disturbances — any compliance or missed step compounds through all downstream joints. Encoders are a V2 priority.

Hardware-in-loop testing must happen early

Many of the software bugs were only discovered when running against real hardware. Simulation-only development gave false confidence. A motion simulator with realistic timing constraints would have caught the serial buffer overflow much earlier.

Non-planar printing needs a dedicated slicer ecosystem

Adapting existing planar slicers for non-planar paths is fragile and labor-intensive. The biggest multiplier for this project would be a purpose-built non-planar slicer with built-in singularity avoidance and orientation optimization.
