Chapter 12 — Roadmap

Future Scope

The current system establishes proof of concept for desktop 6-axis FDM printing. The roadmap extends this foundation toward closed-loop intelligence, richer material capability, automated non-planar slicing, and scaled platforms.

Development Roadmap

✓
v1.0 — Current

Proof of Concept Platform

Open-loop stepper control, analytical IK, PLA/PETG printing, manual non-planar G-code, 40mm/s max speed, ±0.4mm accuracy. Demonstrated non-planar printing and support-free overhangs.

v2.0 — Near Term ~12 months

Closed-Loop Control + Vision Calibration

Add encoder feedback to all joints for closed-loop PID position control. Integrate a monocular camera on the end-effector for real-time TCP position verification using marker-based pose estimation. Target accuracy: ±0.15mm. Also add heated enclosure for ABS/PC printing.

v3.0 — Mid Term ~24 months

Non-Planar Slicer + Multi-Material

Develop or integrate a purpose-built non-planar slicer with automated curved surface detection, stress-aligned toolpath generation, and built-in singularity avoidance. Add a dual-extruder end-effector for multi-material printing (support material + structural material, or two-color).

v4.0 — Long Term ~36+ months

Scaled Platform + High-Performance Materials

Scale the arm to ~600mm reach for printing automotive and aerospace components. Carbon fiber reinforced links, servo-driven joints with harmonic drive reduction for ±0.05mm accuracy. Integrate high-temperature extruder for PEEK, Ultem, and continuous fiber composites.

Vision-Based Closed-Loop Correction

v2.0 Priority

A camera mounted near the nozzle continuously monitors the printed bead using structured light or line laser profilometry. Detected errors — width deviations, gaps, bead height anomalies — feed back into a real-time controller that adjusts feed rate, print speed, or nozzle Z offset within the current layer.

Automated Non-Planar Slicing

v3.0 Priority

Current non-planar toolpaths are manually specified. A purpose-built slicer would automatically: (1) compute surface normals on the STL, (2) generate conformal toolpaths following the surface, (3) optimize nozzle orientations to avoid singularities, and (4) schedule extrusion rate for non-uniform bead widths on curved surfaces.

Multi-Material Printing

v3.0 Priority

A toolchanger mechanism on the J6 flange allows swapping between two or more extruder assemblies during a print. Applications: (1) water-soluble PVA support material for complex overhangs, (2) dual-color aesthetic printing, (3) rigid + flexible material combinations (PLA skeleton + TPU damping layer). The tool change sequence is integrated into the G-code pipeline.

Scale-Up — Industrial 600mm Arm

v4.0 Priority

Scaling the arm to 600mm reach unlocks printing of car body panels, composite brackets, and medical implants. The kinematic architecture scales linearly — the IK and motion planning software is unchanged. Link materials must shift to carbon fiber tubes with bonded aluminum joints. Joint actuators upgrade to brushless DC servos with harmonic drive reduction for <0.01° backlash.

The Bigger Picture

This project demonstrates that desktop-scale 6-axis FDM printing is not just theoretically sound but practically achievable with student budgets. The core insight — that a serial chain arm's spatial freedom can fundamentally change what additive manufacturing can produce — remains valid regardless of the current accuracy and speed limitations.

As non-planar slicing matures, closed-loop sensing becomes cheaper, and high-performance filaments more accessible, this class of machine will move from research curiosity to a genuine alternative to both traditional CNC subtractive manufacturing and planar FDM for complex organic geometries.
