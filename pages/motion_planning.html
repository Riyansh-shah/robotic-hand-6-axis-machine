Chapter 05 — Path & Trajectory

Motion Planning

Converting G-code print paths into synchronized 6-axis joint trajectories — the bridge between slicing software and motor commands. Covering interpolation strategies, velocity profiling, and the critical constraint of maintaining constant nozzle speed for uniform extrusion.

Full Planning Pipeline

1

G-code Parsing → Cartesian Waypoints

The standard G-code output from any slicer (Cura, PrusaSlicer) is parsed line by line. G1 move commands are extracted as Cartesian waypoints (X, Y, Z, E) along with feed-rate F. For non-planar paths, the slicer also outputs nozzle orientation vectors (I, J, K) for each waypoint.

2

Waypoint Densification

Long linear segments are subdivided into sub-waypoints spaced ≤1mm apart. This is required because joint-space interpolation between two widely separated Cartesian points does not follow a straight Cartesian line — the TCP traces a curved arc. Fine subdivision ensures the actual path stays within 0.1mm of the intended straight-line segment.

3

Inverse Kinematics → Joint Angle Array

Each densified waypoint is passed through the analytical IK solver. The result is a time-series of joint angle arrays [θ₁…θ₆] for every waypoint. Configuration selection (elbow up vs. down) is handled greedily — the solution closest to the previous configuration is always chosen to minimize joint motion.

4

Trajectory Timing — Constant TCP Speed

Time is assigned to each waypoint such that the Cartesian speed of the TCP remains constant at the G-code feedrate F. Since joint velocities are non-linear functions of TCP velocity, this requires computing the Jacobian at each waypoint: q̇ = J⁻¹(q) · ẋ_tcp. Joint speed limits are then checked and the feedrate is locally reduced if any joint would exceed its maximum velocity.

5

Cubic Spline Smoothing → Motor Commands

The discrete joint angle series is fit with a piecewise cubic spline in joint space. The spline provides continuous position, velocity, and acceleration at the 1kHz control rate. The smoothed trajectory is then streamed to the controller as absolute joint angle commands at 1ms intervals.

Cartesian Linear Interpolation (LSPB)

The TCP moves in a straight line in Cartesian space from waypoint A to B. Orientation is spherically interpolated (SLERP on the rotation matrix). At each time step, IK is called to convert the Cartesian pose to joint angles.

Exact straight-line TCP path — ideal for perimeter walls

Orientation continuously controlled — nozzle stays normal to surface

IK called at every timestep — computationally heavier

Joint-Space Interpolation (JTRAJ)

Joint angles are interpolated directly from the start configuration to the end configuration as smooth cubic polynomials. No IK calls during motion — fast and simple.

Low compute — perfect for travel moves between prints

TCP traces a curved, unpredictable arc — never used for printing moves

Nozzle speed is not constant — would create uneven extrusion

Trapezoidal Velocity Profile

For each print segment, a trapezoidal velocity profile (also called LSPB — Linear Segments with Parabolic Blends) is applied. The nozzle accelerates from rest, cruises at the target feedrate, then decelerates to rest. This avoids sudden velocity discontinuities that cause vibration-induced print artifacts.

Acceleration Phase: TCP speed ramps up at a constant rate (max acceleration = 500 mm/s²) until target feedrate is reached. Joint accelerations are held below their individual torque-limited maxima during this phase.

Cruise Phase: Constant TCP speed. Joint velocities vary smoothly as the arm changes configuration, all within their mechanical limits.

Deceleration Phase: Symmetric to acceleration. Extrusion rate is commanded to ramp down proportionally so the extruder decelerates at the same ratio as the TCP — preventing blobs at path endpoints.

-- Trapezoidal profile parameters --
max_tcp_vel = 80 mm/s (print)
max_tcp_vel = 200 mm/s (travel)
max_accel = 500 mm/s²
jerk_limit = 2000 mm/s³

-- Joint limits (after gearbox) --
J1_max_vel = 120 °/s
J2_max_vel = 100 °/s
J3_max_vel = 120 °/s
J4-6_max_vel = 150 °/s

Extrusion Synchronization

Extruder E-steps are commanded proportionally to TCP speed so that volumetric flow rate stays constant: E_rate = F_tcp × (π·r²·layer_h) / (π·r_fil²). Any feedrate reduction due to joint limits automatically reduces E_rate by the same factor.
