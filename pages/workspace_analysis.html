Chapter 04 — Spatial Capability

Workspace Analysis

Characterizing the three-dimensional volume the arm's nozzle can reach, identifying dead zones and singularities, and mapping the effective print volume within that workspace.

Reachable Workspace — Toroidal Shell

The full reachable workspace of a 6R arm is not a simple sphere. It forms a hollow toroidal (donut-shaped) shell. The outer radius is bounded by the maximum reach of the fully extended arm (J2 and J3 both at maximum), and the inner radius is the minimum reach when the arm is fully retracted.

Practically, the workspace is further constrained by joint mechanical limits (physical stops prevent the full theoretical range). After applying joint limits, the effective workspace becomes an irregular swept volume — still toroidal in cross-section but with angular gaps in the azimuthal direction from the J1 base limits.

For 3D printing, we restrict operation to the "Goldilocks Zone" — a cylindrical sub-volume at radii 150mm to 300mm from the base center and heights 20mm to 280mm above the base plate, where the arm maintains good stiffness and manageable joint velocities.

Singularities & Dead Zones

Singularities are configurations where the Jacobian loses rank — the arm loses at least one DOF and cannot move the TCP in certain directions, regardless of joint velocities. All 6-axis arms have three types:

Shoulder Singularity

Occurs when the TCP lies directly above or below J1 — the wrist center lies on the J1 axis. J1 and J4 become collinear and the arm cannot control the azimuthal direction. Detection: |Wcx| + |Wcy| < ε.

Condition: r = √(Wcx²+Wcy²) ≈ 0

Elbow Singularity

Occurs when the arm is fully extended or fully retracted — J3 is at 0° or 180°. The elbow loses the ability to change reach. This creates a boundary "shell" in the workspace where path planning must avoid straight-through moves.

Condition: D = ±1 in IK formula

Wrist Singularity

Occurs when J5 = 0° — the J4 and J6 axes become collinear and only their sum/difference can be controlled, not each individually. The arm can still reach the position but cannot control azimuthal orientation. Most problematic for non-planar print paths with vertical nozzle orientation.

Condition: sin(θ₅) ≈ 0

Singularity Avoidance Strategy

Pre-flight Check: Before executing any toolpath, the IK solver checks all waypoints for proximity to singularities. Waypoints within 5° of a singular configuration are flagged.

Path Re-routing: Flagged segments are re-routed via an intermediate waypoint that arcs around the singularity, keeping J5 >10° from zero throughout the print path.

Damped Least Squares (DLS): Near singularities, the numerical fallback uses DLS Jacobian inversion with a damping factor λ=0.05 to prevent joint velocity blow-up while sacrificing a small amount of TCP accuracy.

Speed Limiting: When |sin(θ₅)| < 0.15, the system automatically caps the commanded joint velocities to 30% of normal to prevent sudden large joint motions.
