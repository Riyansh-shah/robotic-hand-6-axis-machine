Chapter 07 — Deposition Hardware

Extruder & Material

The end-effector is where mechanics meets chemistry. Choices of filament type, extrusion mechanism, temperature profile, and physical mounting all directly impact print quality — especially when the hotend must move through the full 6DOF range of the wrist.

Filament Selection & Properties

PLA+ (Primary)

Recommended
Print Temp200–215°C
Bed Temp60°C
Density1.24 g/cm³
Tensile Strength~60 MPa
Diameter1.75mm ±0.02

Low warping, good layer adhesion, adequate for validation prints. Low hygroscopicity means it prints consistently without drying. Chosen for all benchmark tests.

PETG (Secondary)

Print Temp230–245°C
Bed Temp80°C
Density1.27 g/cm³
Tensile Strength~50 MPa
Elongation~170%

Better impact resistance and chemical resistance than PLA. Slightly more stringing — requires retraction tuning. Used for functional structural arm parts.

ABS (Future)

Print Temp230–250°C
Bed Temp100–110°C
Density1.05 g/cm³
Tensile Strength~40 MPa
Heat Deflection98°C

Highest temperature resistance. Significant warping on open-frame printer — requires enclosure. Planned for Version 2 with enclosed print area.

Direct Drive vs. Bowden — The Decision

Chosen: Direct Drive

In a direct-drive setup, the extruder motor sits directly on the hotend carriage — in our case, directly on the J6 tool flange. The filament path from drive gears to nozzle is <30mm.

Excellent retraction control — short, precise filament retraction eliminates stringing on travel moves across the non-planar workspace

Compatible with flexible filaments (TPU) if needed in future

No Bowden tube flex — eliminates pressure variation lag during deceleration

Additional ~180g mass on end-effector — increases J5/J6 torque requirement

Motor cable must flex through full wrist range — strain-relief critical

Rejected: Bowden

A Bowden setup places the extruder motor at the base of the arm and drives filament through a long PTFE tube to the hotend. The tube must flex through the full range of all 6 joints.

Lighter end-effector — reduces J5/J6 torque by ~180g

PTFE tube routing through 6 moving joints is mechanically complex — kinking risk

Variable tube length and angle changes tube compliance unpredictably, creating pressure transients during direction changes

Poor retraction control at varying tube orientations — unacceptable stringing

Temperature Control

Hotend: All-metal V6 clone hotend with 40W heater cartridge and 100K NTC thermistor. PID-controlled to ±0.5°C. Hardened nozzle (tungsten steel, 0.4mm) used for longevity with composite filaments.

Heat Creep Prevention: The heatsink fan runs continuously whenever the hotend is above 50°C. The heatsink is a full-copper V6 type, chosen for 30% better thermal conductivity vs. aluminum — critical when the arm holds still at a location while the next G-code segment is computed.

Part Cooling Fan: 5015 centrifugal fan (blower) mounted on a custom duct that directs airflow at the print regardless of nozzle orientation. Flow rate ~3.5 CFM at 12V.

Mounting Challenges

Weight Budget: The entire end-effector (motor, hotend, fans, wiring) must stay under 250g to preserve J5 torque margins. Current assembly: 218g. Every component was weighed; aluminum parts replaced plastic only where structurally required.

Cable Management: Six cable runs (heater 2-wire, thermistor 2-wire, part fan 2-wire, stepper 4-wire, PTFE tube, limit switch 2-wire) must flex through ±120° J5 and ±360° J6 rotation. Self-coiling polyurethane drag chain handles flexing without fatigue.

Vibration Isolation: Rubber grommets under the stepper motor and hotend mount dampen vibration at high-speed traversals, reducing ringing artifacts on print surfaces.
