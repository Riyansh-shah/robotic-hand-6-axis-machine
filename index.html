Chapter 01 — The Core Problem

Why Cartesian Printing Cannot Scale.

A standard 3-axis printer moves in X, Y, and Z independently. Every layer is flat. Curved surfaces become staircases. Overhangs need supports that get thrown away. Layers bond poorly at their weakest axis. It's a geometry problem that more resolution cannot fix.

Staircase Surfaces

Visible layer artifacts on any organic geometry.

Layer Delamination

Z-axis tensile strength 40–60% below X/Y.

Support Waste

Up to 30% material wasted on removable supports.

IK — Damped Least Squares

The Jacobian maps Cartesian velocity to joint velocity. We invert it with Levenberg-Marquardt damping (λ=0.01) to avoid near-singular configurations at wrist and elbow.

Deep dive →

Trajectory — Trapezoidal Profile

Joint-space interpolation with per-axis velocity (v_max = 1.5 rad/s) and acceleration (a_max = 3.0 rad/s²) limits. Collision detection runs before any command is sent.

Deep dive →

Control — Serial Protocol

115200 baud UART to the microcontroller. Each frame sends 6 target joint angles. The firmware handles step pulse generation and limit-switch homing.
