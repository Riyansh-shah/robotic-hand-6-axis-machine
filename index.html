6-Axis A Desktop Robotic Platform for Seamless Non-Planar 3D Printing

"This project aims to develop a desktop-scale, 6-axis robotic 3D printer using optimized conventional CAD design to minimize arm mass and maximize rigidity. The primary objective is to prove that a 6-axis articulated motion system can produce seamless, high-strength organic parts that are impossible to manufacture on traditional 3-axis Cartesian printers."

Problem Statement

The Challenge Traditional 3D printers are limited to 2.5D (layer-by-layer) movement, which creates weak points between layers and visible "staircase" artifacts on curved surfaces.

The Necessity For complex aerospace or medical components, these layer lines are not just ugly—they are structural failures. Current 5-axis solutions are either too expensive for students or lack the rotational freedom to print completely seamless "closed-loop" geometries.

Literature Review / Market Research

Existing Solutions: Analysis of open-source projects like Thor and AR4 shows that while 3D-printed 6-axis arms exist, they are often too heavy for high-speed precision printing.

Market Gap: Industrial 6-axis printers (like ABB or KUKA systems) cost over $30,000, leaving a gap for a "desktop-class" hybrid machine.

Research Gap / Innovation

Weight Optimization: Unlike previous projects, this uses optimized conventional CAD design for bionic, lattice-based "bones" to reduce inertia.

True Non-Planar Slicing: Innovates with Continuous 5-Axis toolpaths (nozzle always perpendicular to surface) for superior strength and no visible Z-seams.
